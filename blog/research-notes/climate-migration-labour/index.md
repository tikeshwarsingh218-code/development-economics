### Climate, Migration, and Local Labour Markets

Climate and weather shocks can affect labour markets through several channels. A poor agricultural season can reduce household income and demand for local labour, while extreme weather can damage homes, infrastructure, and productive assets. Households may respond by changing how much they work, what activities they undertake, or whether someone migrates.

This makes migration one possible way households respond to economic and environmental shocks. But migration itself has costs, and the ability to move may depend on credit, social networks, information, and opportunities at the destination. I am interested in how environmental shocks interact with these constraints and shape labour-market decisions.
