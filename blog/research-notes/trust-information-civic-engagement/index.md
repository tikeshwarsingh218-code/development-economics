### Trust, Information, and Civic Engagement

Citizens often have to make decisions about government without having complete information about what public institutions are doing. They may know that a programme exists without knowing how it works, who is eligible, or whether implementation has taken place in their area. The quality and accessibility of information can therefore shape how people perceive and interact with the state.

This makes trust an interesting outcome to study. Better information may increase confidence when government performance matches what citizens are told, but it may also change perceptions when people discover gaps between policy and implementation. I am interested in this relationship between information, trust, and civic engagement.
