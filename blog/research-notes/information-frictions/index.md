### Information Frictions in Labour Markets

Markets work differently when people do not have complete information. A worker looking for a job may not know which employers pay on time, provide reasonable working conditions, or keep their promises. Similarly, an employer may have limited information about a worker's skills, reliability, or previous experience.

These information gaps can affect who meets whom in the labour market and which jobs workers accept. They can also create a role for information-sharing institutions, recommendations, networks, or other mechanisms that help workers and firms make better decisions. This is one reason I find information frictions useful for thinking about informal employment and migration.
