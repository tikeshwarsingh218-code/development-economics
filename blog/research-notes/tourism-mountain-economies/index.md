### Tourism, Employment, and Mountain Economies

Tourism can be an important source of employment in mountain regions, but its effects are not uniform. It can create demand for accommodation, transport, food, construction, and local services while also increasing pressure on land, infrastructure, water, and other resources. The interesting question is therefore not simply whether tourism creates jobs, but what kinds of jobs it creates and who benefits from them.

Mountain economies also face geographic constraints that shape how local economies develop. Remoteness can make some activities expensive while making natural and cultural amenities economically valuable. I am interested in how tourism interacts with local labour markets, migration decisions, household livelihoods, and the broader development of mountain regions.
