### State Capacity and Everyday Public Services

State capacity is often discussed in terms of taxation, law enforcement, or the ability of governments to implement large policies. But capacity is also visible in much smaller interactions: whether a public institution has the resources it needs, whether records are maintained, whether staff are present, and whether a policy actually reaches the people it is intended to serve.

Fieldwork has made me interested in this gap between policy on paper and implementation on the ground. Looking closely at local institutions can reveal administrative constraints that are difficult to see in aggregate data. It also raises questions about how budgets, incentives, information, and accountability interact in the delivery of public services.
