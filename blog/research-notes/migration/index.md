### Why Do Workers Migrate?

Migration is often described as a response to differences in wages and employment opportunities, but the decision to migrate is rarely that simple. Households also consider the cost of moving, access to information, family responsibilities, social networks, housing, credit, and the risks associated with finding work in another place.

This makes migration closely connected to other areas of development economics. A change in rural employment opportunities, access to credit, public works, or local infrastructure can potentially affect whether people move. I am interested in how these constraints interact and how migration changes both the places workers leave and the places where they work.
