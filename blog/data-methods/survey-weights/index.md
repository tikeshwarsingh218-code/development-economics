### Survey weights and complex survey design

Survey weights account for differences in the probability that observations are selected into a sample. They allow researchers to construct estimates that better represent the population covered by the survey. Weights can arise from sampling probabilities, nonresponse adjustments, and other features of survey design.

Weights are only one part of a complex survey design. Clustering and stratification can also affect the calculation of standard errors. Researchers therefore need to understand the complete survey design rather than applying weights without considering how the sample was constructed.
